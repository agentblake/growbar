# Money-tree light preset: evidence and design

This preset is for an indoor money tree (*Pachira aquatica*, including plants sold
under the closely related money-tree trade name) illuminated with an Aputure
INFINIBAR PB12. It is an evidence-based starting point, not a claim that a cinema
light can deliver a calibrated horticultural spectrum.

## What the evidence supports

- A controlled *Pachira aquatica* study found photosynthetic light-compensation
  points around 8–16 µmol·m⁻²·s⁻¹ and saturation points around 213–259
  µmol·m⁻²·s⁻¹. Plants were commercially produced under daily maximum PPFD values
  of 285, 350, or 550 µmol·m⁻²·s⁻¹; the authors recommended the 285–350 range for
  production and later indoor performance. This does not mean an indoor plant
  needs a constant 350 PPFD all day. [Li et al., HortScience 44(5), 2009](https://doi.org/10.21273/HORTSCI.44.5.1291)
- A newer 191-day controlled study directly compared *P. aquatica* under white
  LEDs at 0.22, 0.43, and 1.30 DLI. All but one root-rot casualty sustained
  growth. Higher intensity produced thicker leaves, while leaf count, leaf area,
  dry weight, and relative growth rate did not differ significantly for Pachira.
  This establishes that 1.30 DLI is useful indoor light, but it does not establish
  a species optimum. [Sugano et al., Scientific Reports 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-67877-y)
- Extension guidance classifies foliage houseplants broadly around 3–6 DLI for
  low light and 6–10 DLI for medium light. DLI is PPFD × hours × 0.0036.
  [Iowa State University Extension](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/growing-indoor-plants-under-supplemental-lights/important-considerations-providing-supplemental-light-indoor-plants)
- Money trees prefer bright, indirect light and can scorch in direct sunlight.
  [North Carolina State Extension](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/pachira-aquatica/common-name/guiana-chestnut/)
- Red photons are photosynthetically efficient, but healthy plant form generally
  needs some blue and benefits from a broad spectrum. Green photons also penetrate
  deeper into leaves and canopies. White and red/blue treatments produced broadly
  comparable growth in several ornamental species when PPFD was held constant.
  [Park and Runkle, PLOS One 2018](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0202386),
  [Kusuma et al., Plant Science 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plantsci.2020.110456)
- Most plants need darkness and general indoor-plant guidance recommends no more
  than 16 total illuminated hours per day.
  [University of Maryland Extension](https://www.extension.umd.edu/resource/lighting-indoor-plants)
- Evening bedroom light can suppress melatonin, increase alertness, delay circadian
  timing, and interfere with later sleep. The response depends on spectrum,
  intensity, duration, and the person.
  [Santhi et al., Scientific Reports 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5536841/),
  [Cajochen et al., Journal of Applied Physiology 2011](https://doi.org/10.1152/japplphysiol.00165.2011)
- The PB12 is an RGBWW cinema fixture. Aputure specifies 2,000–10,000K CCT, a 120°
  beam, 27W maximum output, 2,077 lumens, and white/color lux measurements, but no
  PPFD map or red-emitter peak wavelength.
  [Aputure PB12 specifications](https://aputure.com/EN-US/products/infinibar-pb12)

## Why this schedule

The light runs from 09:15 to 23:15: a 14-hour photoperiod followed by ten hours of
uninterrupted darkness. It begins at wake time and ends three hours before a 02:15
bedtime. That accommodates the plant without exposing the sleeper to the PB12
during sleep or the last three hours before bed.

No published Pachira experiment identifies a single optimum DLI for a mature indoor
plant. The evidence supports a lower and more cautious starting point than the old
6–9 DLI recommendation: aim initially for 2–4 total DLI at the canopy, including
window light. If the plant is acclimated, growing actively, watered appropriately,
and shows no light or heat stress, increasing gradually toward 4–6 DLI is reasonable.
For a 14-hour day, 2–4 DLI corresponds to an average 40–80 PPFD and 4–6 DLI to
80–120 PPFD. A PAR meter or quantum sensor is the reliable way to resolve the total.

Most of the day uses 5,600K white at full fixture intensity because it preserves a
broad spectrum and the PB12 has strong published output near daylight CCT. A brief
6,500K morning step supplies a blue-balanced period. At 18:45 the preset changes to
3,200K at full intensity: a broad warm-white spectrum that is relatively red-rich
without depriving the plant of blue and green photons. It is also more comfortable
for a bedroom than blue-rich light late in the evening.

This replaces the old HSI-red step. Aputure measures the PB12 at 1,410 lux in 3,200K
white at 0.5 m, versus only 227 lux in pure red. Lux cannot compare plant photon
output across spectra, and a 35%-saturated HSI color is not the same as pure red,
but those figures make clear that the bar is designed to deliver much more light in
white mode. There is no Pachira-specific evidence that a timed monochromatic red
pulse outperforms continuous broad-spectrum light. Warm white is therefore the more
defensible way to include a red-rich phase with this particular fixture.

Using Aputure's center illuminance figures and the 0.01365 PPFD-per-lux conversion
measured for the white LEDs in Sugano et al. gives a rough estimate near 1 DLI from
the PB12 alone at 0.5 m over this preset. That is an approximation, not a calibration,
because the two fixtures have different spectra and the PB12 is a long line source.
Window light and closer placement can raise the total substantially.

## Setup and adjustment

1. Place the bar above or to the side of the whole canopy so every leaf is lit as
   evenly as practical. Start around 30–50 cm from the nearest leaves, with no
   fabric grid or softbox, then measure and adjust.
2. Measure PPFD at the top, middle, and edges of the canopy. Move the PB12 until the
   total daily dose, including daylight, is initially around 2–4 DLI. Do not use lux
   to compare different color modes; lux weights light for human vision, not plants.
3. Include window light when assessing daily dose. If the plant already receives
   strong daylight, reduce PB12 intensity or distance it farther away.
4. Acclimate a plant that previously had dim light over two to three weeks,
   initially using lower intensity or greater distance. Increase exposure by about
   15–20% every 4–7 days and inspect new growth rather than one day of leaf movement.
5. If growth stretches or leaf drop persists without watering/root problems,
   increase DLI gradually. If leaves bleach, curl, or become hot, increase distance
   or reduce intensity. Keep the PB12 dry; its published rating is IP20.

The schedule controls timing and fixture settings. Distance, canopy coverage,
window light, temperature, watering, and the plant's prior acclimation remain just
as important as the number of schedule steps.
